\begin{abstract}
This note describes the time-domain full-waveform inversion (FWI) implemented in
\code{invert\_ring.py}, which recovers a two-dimensional sound-speed map from
ring-array ultrasound traces, and \code{totalseg\_to\_speed.py}, which builds
abdominal sound-speed maps from an open, expert-segmented CT dataset and a
table of tissue acoustic properties. On a TotalSegmentator abdomen
(1450--3750~m/s, 128 elements), three-stage FWI (10--50, 50--125 and
100--250~kHz on 4.0, 1.83 and 1.5~mm grids, with total-variation
regularization), started from uniform water, recovers the organs, fat, muscle
and bone: the interior RMS error falls from 293 to 84~m/s and the soft-tissue
RMS error from 53 to 14~m/s (4~m/s more than 10~mm from bone; liver 2.5,
kidney 4.4~m/s), and the median bone speed reaches 3785 against a true
3750~m/s. Two implementation bugs had masked this: the multiscale handoff
rescaled the model by array shape although the CT stages cover different fields
of view, and batched runs never excluded the transmitter's own channel. The
note records both and the runs that exposed them.
\end{abstract}

\section{Forward model}

The forward model is the 2D constant-density acoustic wave equation,
discretized with second-order finite differences in space and time (FDTD).
Sound speed $c(\mathbf{x})$ is the only material property; density and
attenuation are constant:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial t^2} = c(\mathbf{x})^2\,\nabla^2 u
  + s(t)\,\delta(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_{\mathrm{tx}}).
\end{equation}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Time stepping.} A leapfrog scheme advances the pressure,
    $u^{n+1} = 2u^n - u^{n-1} + \Delta t^2 c^2 L[u^n]$, where $L$ is the
    5-point Laplacian applied as a $3\times3$ convolution.
  \item \textbf{Stability.} The step obeys the 2D CFL bound
    $c_{\max}\,\Delta t\,\sqrt{1/\Delta x^2 + 1/\Delta y^2} < 1$. The code uses a
    CFL number of 0.45, with $c_{\max}$ the larger of the true medium maximum
    and the upper reconstruction bound.
  \item \textbf{Boundaries.} First-order Mur absorbing conditions on all four
    edges; corners average the $x$- and $y$-direction updates.
  \item \textbf{Sources and receivers.} A transmitter is a soft (additive) point
    source at its nearest grid node. Receivers sample pressure by bilinear
    interpolation, so element positions are not locked to pixel centres.
  \item \textbf{Execution.} Fields stay on one PyTorch device (CPU, CUDA or
    Apple MPS). Independent transmitter shots are advanced together as a batch,
    and on CUDA the step is compiled with \code{torch.compile}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Simulation setup}
\label{sec:setup}

A ring of point transducers surrounds the object; each element both transmits
and receives. One element fires a chirp per shot and every element records,
giving an $N \times N \times$ time dataset. Two configurations are used: the
default phantom setup (200~mm field of view, 70~mm ring) and the abdominal
setup, which scales the grid and ring to fit a 0.3~m-wide torso.

\subsection{Abdominal sound-speed model from an open segmentation}
\label{sec:totalseg}

The medium is one axial slice of a public, expert-segmented CT, converted by
\code{totalseg\_to\_speed.py}. Organs, vessels, bowel, bone and the deep back
muscles come from the segmentation; only pixels no mask covers are classified
by Hounsfield units (HU).

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Data.} The \emph{Small subset of TotalSegmentator Dataset}
    v2.0.1 \cite{totalseg_data, wasserthal2023} holds 102 CT volumes resampled to
    1.5~mm, each with masks of 117 structures (CC BY 4.0). \code{--fetch}
    downloads a single subject out of the 3.2~GB zip with HTTP range requests
    (44~MB for subject s1366).
  \item \textbf{Subject choice.} Of the 54 subjects whose masks include the
    main upper-abdominal organs, the cleanest were screened by compressed bytes per voxel,
    then by the HU standard deviation inside the liver after removing a
    $5^3$-voxel local mean. Subject s1366 has 14.7~HU of noise, against 140~HU
    for the noisiest (low-dose) scan, s0407, which was rejected.
  \item \textbf{Slice choice.} Among slices showing all ten key structures
    (liver, spleen, both kidneys, pancreas, stomach, aorta, inferior vena cava,
    both deep back muscles), the one with the least gas inside the body is
    used: slice 259, at the T12/L1 level, with 2.5\% gas.
  \item \textbf{Tissue classes.} Masks are merged into the classes of
    Table~\ref{tab:tissues}. Unlabelled body pixels are classified by HU after
    a $5\times5$ median filter: fat ($-190$ to $-30$), gas ($<-190$) or other
    soft tissue. Gas, which the scalar fluid model cannot represent, becomes
    water; inside segmented solid organs low HU is treated as noise, not gas.
    The body is the largest connected region above $-500$~HU, which excludes
    the arms and the scanner table.
  \item \textbf{Acoustic properties per class.} Each class points to one row
    of \code{abdominal\_acoustic\_parameters.csv} (Table~\ref{tab:acoustic}),
    compiled from \cite{duck1990, szabo, mast2000}. The script writes speed,
    density, power-law attenuation and $B/A$ maps; the present solvers are
    lossless and constant-density, so only speed enters them. Gas is modelled
    as water rather than 343~m/s air: without a density contrast the scalar
    model cannot reproduce the near-total reflection at a gas boundary.
  \item \textbf{Embedding.} \code{load\_ct\_ring\_grid} centres the body,
    resamples it to each stage's grid with nearest-neighbour sampling, pads the
    outside with 1480~m/s water, and places the ring 10~mm outside the body
    with an 8-cell margin to the grid edge.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Inversion algorithm}
\label{sec:algorithm}

The inversion minimizes the least-squares misfit between simulated and observed
traces over slowness squared $m = 1/c^2$, using an exact discrete adjoint for
the gradient and first-order updates. Everything below is implemented in
\code{invert\_ring.py} and \code{fdtd2d/inversion/}.

\subsection{Unknowns and constraints}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Parameter.} $m = 1/c^2$ on every pixel inside an interior disk
    $\Omega$ of radius (ring radius $-$ margin), with a 2-pixel margin by
    default. Outside $\Omega$, $c$ is fixed at the coupling-medium speed. The
    wave operator is linear in $c^2 = 1/m$, which keeps the gradient simple.
  \item \textbf{Bounds.} After every update $m$ is projected onto
    $[1/c_{\max}^2,\, 1/c_{\min}^2]$ and the exterior is reset to the
    background.
  \item \textbf{Starting model.} Homogeneous coupling medium (1500 or
    1480~m/s) inside the mask. For diagnosis only,
    \code{-{}-start-blurred-truth $\sigma$} starts from the true slowness
    blurred by a Gaussian of $\sigma$~mm.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Objective}

\begin{equation}
  J(m) = \frac{1}{2}\sum_{s \in S}\sum_{r \neq s}\sum_{n}
  \left(d^{\,\mathrm{pred}}_{s,r,n}(m) - d^{\,\mathrm{obs}}_{s,r,n}\right)^2
  + \frac{\alpha}{2}\,\lVert \nabla m \rVert^2_{\Omega}
  + \beta \sum_{\Omega} \left(\sqrt{\lvert \nabla m \rvert^2 + \varepsilon^2} - \varepsilon\right).
\end{equation}
$S$ is the set of active transmitters, $r$ runs over receivers and $n$ over
time samples. The transmitter's own channel is excluded because a point
source's direct pressure is a poorly conditioned datum. (In batched runs
before commit \code{d519e92} this exclusion silently failed; see
Section~\ref{sec:s1366}.) The Tikhonov term penalizes squared differences
between neighbouring pixels inside $\Omega$, with weight $\alpha$
(\code{-{}-reg}). The total-variation (TV) term penalizes the magnitude of the
forward-difference gradient, smoothed by $\varepsilon$ (\code{-{}-tv-eps},
default $10^{-9}$ in units of $m$, about 2~m/s at 1500~m/s), with weight
$\beta$ (\code{-{}-tv}). Because TV grows linearly with a jump, it suppresses
speckle while leaving sharp organ and bone boundaries much cheaper than the
quadratic Tikhonov term does. Both weights default to 0 and can be set per
stage.

Observed data are synthetic: at the start of each stage the same solver
simulates all $N$ transmitters through the true medium, without noise.

\subsection{Adjoint-state gradient}

For each active shot the forward pass stores the full wavefield history $u^n$.
The adjoint field $\lambda$ is then stepped backward in time with the transposed
operator $L(c^2\,\cdot)$, driven by the residuals scattered onto the grid by
the transpose of the bilinear receiver sampling. The gradient with respect to
$c^2$ accumulates as
\begin{equation}
  g_{c^2} = \Delta t^2 \sum_{n} \lambda^{n} \odot L\!\left[u^{\,n-1}\right],
  \qquad
  \frac{\partial J}{\partial m} = -\frac{g_{c^2}}{m^2} + \nabla_m R(m),
\end{equation}
where $R$ is the sum of the Tikhonov and TV terms. The gradient is set to zero
outside the mask. With \code{-{}-store-every $k$} the sum runs over every
$k$-th forward step, weighted by $k$, and \code{-{}-wave-dtype float16} stores
the forward fields at half precision; on the test problem these change the
gradient by $2\times10^{-7}$ ($k = 4$) and $9\times10^{-5}$ (float16). The
adjoint reuses the forward Mur
boundary update, which is not its exact transpose; the finite-difference
gradient check (Section~\ref{sec:verification}) bounds the effect of this.

\code{-{}-model neural} replaces the per-pixel unknowns with the weights of a
Fourier-feature coordinate network; the same adjoint gradient is passed through
the network by the chain rule.

\subsection{Shot sub-sampling}

Each gradient evaluation uses $K$ transmitters drawn at random without
replacement (\code{-{}-n-shots}, default 4; \code{-{}-shot-seed} fixes the
sequence), so one evaluation costs $2K$ wave solves instead of $2N$. Shots run
in GPU batches of up to \code{-{}-parallel-shots}. Its default,
\code{auto}, sizes each stage's batches from free GPU memory and splits the
shots into equal batches; the abdomen run fits all 32 shots in one batch.

\subsection{Multiscale continuation}

\code{-{}-multiscale} runs coarse-to-fine stages on progressively finer grids
(Table~\ref{tab:setup}). Each stage regenerates its own observed data, and the
final $m$ of one stage is transferred to the next by bicubic interpolation in
physical coordinates: the new grid samples the old model at its own cell
centres, with background outside the old field of view. (Resizing by array
shape alone, as the code first did, is correct only when every stage covers
the same field of view; Section~\ref{sec:history}.) \code{-{}-stages} sets how
many stages run (default 3). \code{-{}-coarse-band} sets the first stage's
chirp band (default 25--75~kHz; the abdomen uses 10--50~kHz). Starting at a low
frequency lengthens the period relative to the initial travel-time errors,
which reduces cycle skipping: fitting a trace one period off, which traps
inversions in a wrong model. \code{-{}-n-elements}, \code{-{}-max-iter},
\code{-{}-adam-lr}, \code{-{}-reg} and \code{-{}-tv} take either one value or
one value per stage, and \code{-{}-save-model X.npz} also saves every earlier
stage as \code{X\_stage$k$.npz}.

\section{Implementation and verification}
\label{sec:verification}

The adjoint gradient matches finite differences to second order, and the cost
of one iteration is two wave solves per sampled shot plus the stored wavefield.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Batching.} \code{TorchFDTD2DBatch} advances independent shots
    as one (batch, $n_y$, $n_x$) tensor. Each forward step is fused with the
    receiver gather, and each adjoint step with the residual injection and the
    gradient accumulation, so a time step is one compiled call
    (\code{torch.compile(mode="reduce-overhead")} on CUDA). Against the earlier
    unfused code this is about $9\times$, $6\times$ and $2\times$ faster per
    shot on the three abdomen grids.
  \item \textbf{Memory.} The forward pass keeps the wavefield of every shot in
    the batch for the adjoint: $n_{\mathrm{steps}} \times n_y \times n_x \times 4$
    bytes per shot in float32 (1.95~GB on a $401^2$, 3039-step grid). Storing
    every fourth step in float16 cuts this $8\times$, so all 32 shots of the
    abdomen run fit in one batch on every stage (about 10~GB peak).
  \item \textbf{Gradient check.} \code{python invert\_ring.py -{}-gradient-check
    -{}-dtype float64} compares the adjoint directional derivative
    $g \cdot \delta m$ with central differences on a $41 \times 41$ grid with a
    12~mm, 1800~m/s inclusion (Table~\ref{tab:gradcheck}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Abdomen imaging results}
\label{sec:results}

Three-stage FWI of the segmentation-based s1366 abdomen, started from uniform
1480~m/s water, recovers soft tissue and bone: the interior RMS error falls
from 293.1 to 84.2~m/s and the soft-tissue RMS error from 53.3 to 13.6~m/s.
Section~\ref{sec:history} records the runs that led to this setup, including
the two bugs that had hidden it.

\subsection{Segmentation-based abdomen (TotalSegmentator s1366)}
\label{sec:s1366}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Bug 1: transmitter channel.} Batched runs never excluded the
    transmitter's own channel from the misfit: the zeroing acted on a copy made
    by advanced indexing. It affects the first two rows and the KiTS run
    (Section~\ref{sec:kits}), and was found by a test comparing the batched
    gradient with an unbatched reference.
  \item \textbf{Shots, elements and regularization.} More shots per iteration
    did not remove the artifacts; 256 elements removed the radial streaks near
    the ring. Tikhonov at $10^{15}$ suppressed speckle but charged the true
    bone edges about six times the converged coarse misfit, blocking the bone;
    TV, which charges jumps linearly, replaced it.
  \item \textbf{A misleading diagnostic.} Saving the model after every stage
    showed stages 1 and 2 working and stage 3 producing the same speckled,
    biased model from any input, including a 10~mm-blurred true map
    (Figures~\ref{fig:stages_water} and~\ref{fig:stages_blur}). This was first
    read as cycle skipping at 100--250~kHz, and stage 3 was dropped. A stage-3
    test with steps too small to move any pixel more than about 7~m/s still
    raised the error from 145 to 386~m/s, which ruled that out.
  \item \textbf{Bug 2: stage transfer.} The transfer resized the model by array
    shape, as if every stage covered the same field of view. The s1366 stages
    cover $\pm200$, $\pm183$ and $\pm300$~mm, so stage 2 started from a model
    shrunk by 8.5\% and stage 3 from one magnified $1.64\times$, scoring
    388~m/s before any update. Interpolating in physical coordinates fixed both:
    stage 2 improved from 145 to 119~m/s, and stage 3 now lowers the error from
    140 to 84~m/s on its grid. The phantom stages share one field of view,
    which is why the bug did not show there. The KiTS stages (468, 425 and
    406~mm) were affected too.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Earlier experiment: KiTS-00136 with the HU-threshold model}
\label{sec:kits}

The first abdomen runs used the \code{ct\_to\_speed.py} map of C4KC-KiTS case
KiTS-00136 (categorical mapping, 1478--3476~m/s). Its stages ran at 4.63, 2.12
and 1.01~mm on 468, 425 and 406~mm fields of view, with a 194.4~mm ring of 64
elements (19~mm pitch). Because those fields of view differ, this run also
used the faulty stage transfer of Section~\ref{sec:history}, and it kept each
transmitter's own channel in the misfit. Three-stage FWI recovered the
soft-tissue anatomy and
the outer cortex of the vertebra, but left the vertebral body hollow,
underestimated by up to about 2000~m/s.

\section{Limitations and future work}
\label{sec:future}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Inverse crime.} Observed and predicted data come from the same
    solver on the same grid, with no noise. This is the largest caveat on the
    results: data should next be generated on a finer grid, or with an
    independent solver, with noise added.
  \item \textbf{Grid resolution.} The final stage has only 3.9 points per
    shortest wavelength (limited by the 1.5~mm CT voxels), low for a
    second-order scheme. Numerical dispersion is invisible here only because
    data and model share the grid; real data will need a fourth-order stencil
    or a finer grid.
  \item \textbf{Bone and its surroundings.} Bone reaches its true median speed,
    but its edges and the tissue within 10~mm of it carry most of the remaining
    error (43.7~m/s). A bone-aware prior, a travel-time misfit or more stage-3
    iterations are the next things to try.
  \item \textbf{Run time.} The final run takes an estimated 17~minutes on an
    idle RTX A6000, 60\% of it in stage 3. Fewer shots do not help on these
    grids (8 shots were no faster than 32); checkpointing or a custom kernel
    that advances several time steps per launch would.
  \item \textbf{Element count.} With 128 elements the pitch (8.3~mm) exceeds
    half a wavelength above about 90~kHz. No streaks remain in the final
    result, but 256 elements removed them in earlier runs and should be
    re-tested with the fixed transfer.
  \item \textbf{Physics.} Constant density, no attenuation and no shear waves.
    Bone and gas are only approximated by a scalar acoustic model. The
    density and attenuation maps written by \code{totalseg\_to\_speed.py} are
    not yet used by the solvers.
  \item \textbf{Reproducibility.} The commands and logs of all abdomen runs are
    saved alongside their figures. The KiTS-00136 command was recovered after
    the fact from shell history.
\end{itemize}
